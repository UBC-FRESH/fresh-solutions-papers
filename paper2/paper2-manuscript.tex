\title{FRESH Solutions: a reproducible adapter harness for applied metaheuristic benchmarks}
\author{UBC FRESH Lab\\University of British Columbia}

\begin{abstract}
Applying metaheuristics to hard combinatorial problems is often slowed less by the choice of
metaheuristic than by the engineering around it: problem-specific move representations, exact
incremental state, replayable evidence, stopping rules, and benchmark bookkeeping are usually rebuilt
for each project. FRESH Solutions provides an adapter contract and a reproducible benchmark harness,
together with two reference implementations: quadratic-assignment robust tabu search and
travelling-salesman iterated Lin--Kernighan. We evaluate the references on QAPLIB and TSPLIB and report
the full distribution, including failures. We also run a framework-use check: the QAP tabu trajectory
is reproduced with the incumbent held in the engine's resident-state contract. On 40 runs across four
QAPLIB instances, all runs stopped on the same 100,000-iteration plateau rule under a 1,000,000
iteration cap, all framework objectives matched the standalone and production objectives exactly, and
all incumbents replayed exactly. A separate 20-instance QAPLIB panel, ten seeds per instance, also stopped on patience and replayed
exactly; its mean gap was 0.301\%, while an independent Breakout Local Search baseline reached
0.096\% and 161/200 optima. The framework path cost 1.24 times
the CPU time of the private-array delta path (95\% CI 1.17--1.31). A 10,000-kick-cap TSP campaign with 1,000-kick patience plateau-stopped on all 20 runs;
the runs stopped after 1,000--1,380 kicks and all 20 reached the known optimum. The contribution is the harness, exact replay contract, reference
adapters, and this bounded framework-use result, not a state-of-the-art solver claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords: metaheuristics, benchmark harness, quadratic assignment, travelling salesman, reproducibility}

\section{Introduction}

Operations-research practitioners rarely need a new metaheuristic. They need a reliable way to apply
known methods to a new problem, preserve exact feasibility and objective accounting, and produce
evidence that another person can replay. That work is repetitive and easy to get subtly wrong.
Problem-specific move representations, incremental state, stopping rules, benchmark harnesses, seed
handling, and evidence records are rebuilt for each project.

FRESH Solutions is a response to that engineering problem. It provides a problem-adapter contract,
exact resident state and reversible moves, local and population search paths, checkpointing, exact
replay, convergence records, and public-library reference implementations for QAP and TSP. The paper
asks a narrow engineering question: can a user add a problem-specific neighbourhood or reference
implementation and obtain credible, replayable evidence without rebuilding the surrounding machinery?
We answer it with a bounded framework-use experiment: a QAP tabu path whose incumbent is held in the
engine's resident state reproduces the private-array delta-matrix trajectory exactly, at a measured
state-management cost. We do not claim that the generic simulated-annealing layer is competitive with
the adapter-specific references.

The paper makes four contributions. First, it specifies a small adapter contract that separates a
problem's exact state, moves, and feasibility from the search layer. Second, it specifies a replay and
evidence protocol: an incumbent is not a result unless a new adapter evaluation reproduces the
stored objective and the record names the instance, seed, budget, stopping reason, and provenance.
Third, it provides two public reference implementations, robust tabu for QAP and iterated
Lin--Kernighan for TSP, and reports their full distributions, including tails and cap hits. Fourth, it
reports a convergence-controlled framework-use check for QAP state management and a separate TSP
convergence campaign; both are bounded empirical results, not a general-framework claim.

The scope is deliberately narrow. The QAP convergence panel has four instances; the TSP convergence
panel has four; the broad panels screen many more instances but do not claim per-run convergence. The
external LKH binary is a capability reference and is not bundled. The generic population and
simulated-annealing layers are implemented and tested, but the headline results come from the
adapter-specific reference modules. This is the honest boundary of the current evidence.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews the problem and
benchmarking literature. Section~\ref{sec:framework} describes the adapter, resident state, search
layers, and evidence protocol. Section~\ref{sec:qap} reports the QAP evidence and the framework-use
experiment. Section~\ref{sec:tsp} reports the TSP convergence campaign and the LKH boundary.
Section~\ref{sec:applied} describes the applied case, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses
limitations and the claim boundary.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quadratic assignment}

The quadratic assignment problem assigns facilities to locations while minimising a flow--distance
product \citep{koopmans1957}. It is NP-hard \citep{sahni1976,garey1979} and remains a standard test bed for
metaheuristics because small changes in the flow and distance matrices can produce very different
search landscapes \citep{loiola2007}. QAPLIB is the common public benchmark library
\citep{burkard1997}. Exact methods are limited to modest sizes or special structure, so the literature
is dominated by local search and hybrid methods.

Tabu search is a long-standing metaheuristic \citep{glover1989}; robust tabu search is a standard QAP baseline \citep{taillard1991}. It scans facility swaps, keeps a
tabu tenure for recently changed pairs, and accepts the best non-tabu move even when it worsens the
objective. The reactive variant adapts the tenure to the search history \citep{battiti1994}. More
recent breakout local search and memetic methods combine tabu or descent with perturbation and
population recombination \citep{benlic2013}. Taillard's comparison of iterative searches remains a
useful reminder that restart, perturbation, and acceptance rules can matter as much as the
neighbourhood \citep{taillard1995}. Our QAP adapter follows the robust-tabu move-and-memory idea, with
exact swap deltas and an optional delta-matrix path; it is a reference implementation, not a claim of
state-of-the-art QAP performance.

\subsection{Traveling salesman}

The traveling salesman problem has an equally long heuristic literature. The 2-opt exchange \citep{croes1958}, the Lin--Kernighan
variable-depth exchange \citep{lin1973} remains the foundation of the strongest local-search methods.
Iterated local search adds perturbation to a local optimum and is effective when the perturbation is
matched to the neighbourhood \citep{lourenco2003}. Helsgaun's LKH implementation
\citep{helsgaun2000} and the Concorde branch-and-cut code \citep{applegate2006} are the standard strong
references. TSPLIB provides the public instances \citep{reinelt1991}, and the local-search case study
of Johnson and McGeoch documents the gap between a textbook 2-opt implementation and a carefully
engineered candidate-list method \citep{johnson1997}. We implement a bounded candidate-list
LK-style exchange and use external LKH only as an optional capability reference, not as part of the
package. Our contribution is the adapter and evidence boundary, not a new TSP heuristic.

\subsection{Benchmarking and reproducibility}

Computational experiments with heuristics are easy to make incomparable. Hooker argued that
algorithm comparisons should test hypotheses about why a method works, not just report wins
\citep{hooker1995}. Barr et al.\ proposed a reporting discipline for heuristic experiments
\citep{barr1995}, and McGeoch developed an experimental method for algorithm simulation
\citep{mcgeoch1996}. Beiranvand et al.\ collect best practices for comparing optimization algorithms,
including budget accounting, multiple seeds, and distributional reporting \citep{beiranvand2017}.
COCO provides a platform for comparing continuous optimizers \citep{hansen2020}, and recent benchmarking guidance emphasizes reproducible protocols and distributional reporting \citep{bartz2020}, and irace provides
automatic algorithm configuration with racing \citep{lopez2016}. These systems are stronger than our
harness in their statistical machinery; our focus is the problem-adapter and exact-replay boundary for
discrete, domain-specific models.

Reproducibility in applied optimisation depends on more than a seed. It depends on the compiled
model, candidate order, objective weights, hardware, and replay procedure. The general reproducible
research literature makes the same point for computational science
\citep{peng2011,sandve2013,wilson2014,stodden2016,donoho2009}. Containers and environment capture
reduce the software part of the problem \citep{boettiger2015,kurtzer2017}. Our harness stores the
problem instance identifier, pinned solver commit, seed, budget, stop reason, exact-replay result,
and hardware with each record. The design is deliberately closer to a benchmark harness than to a new
metaheuristic.

\subsection{Software frameworks}

General metaheuristic frameworks usually expose a solution representation, an objective, and a
neighbourhood, then supply search operators \citep{blum2003,hoos2004}. The engineering burden shifts
to the adapter, but the evidence boundary is often left to the user. Benchmarking platforms such as
COCO and configuration tools such as irace solve adjacent problems: COCO standardises the comparison
of continuous optimizers, and irace standardises the tuning of algorithm parameters
\citep{hansen2020,lopez2016}. Neither is a drop-in answer for a compiled spatial model with
domain-specific feasibility and exact replay.

Our adapter contract makes the evidence boundary part of the interface: exact evaluation, reversible
moves, replay, and convergence records are required operations rather than optional utilities. That
choice is the difference between a library of operators and a reproducible benchmark harness. It also
explains the paper's narrow claim. A framework that owns the evidence boundary can be evaluated on
whether the boundary is preserved; a framework that owns the search algorithm must be evaluated on
solution quality against the state of the art. This paper tests the first claim, not the second.

The design is closer in spirit to a laboratory notebook than to a solver. The notebook does not make
the experiment correct by itself, but it makes the experiment inspectable. The adapter is the page
format; the evidence record is the entry; the replay gate is the check that the entry still
reproduces. That metaphor is not a substitute for algorithmic novelty, but it is a useful way to
describe the contribution without inflating it.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Design goals}

The harness is built around four goals. The first is \emph{exactness}: every reported assignment is a
complete discrete assignment, and every objective is computed by the problem adapter, not by a search
heuristic's internal counter. The second is \emph{reversibility}: a move can be applied and undone
without rebuilding the whole assignment, so a local search can test neighbours without allocating a
new state for every candidate. The third is \emph{replayability}: a result is stored with enough
metadata that a third party can re-evaluate the incumbent and recover the same objective and
feasibility. The fourth is \emph{separation}: the search layer sees only the adapter interface, so a
domain-specific neighbourhood can be added without changing the engine.

These goals are deliberately modest. The harness does not attempt to infer a problem's structure,
choose a neighbourhood automatically, or prove optimality. Those are research contributions in their
own right. The harness is the scaffolding that makes such contributions comparable and replayable.

\subsection{Adapter contract}

An adapter supplies discrete variables, their options, a complete initial and random assignment,
exact and batched evaluation, feasibility, mutation and crossover, and reversible moves. The engine
sees only this interface. An assignment is a mapping from every variable to one option; for a
permutation problem, the adapter is responsible for preserving the permutation invariant. A change
records the variable, the option before, the option after, and an optional move kind and segment size.
The adapter's \texttt{apply} and \texttt{undo} operations must be exact inverses on the assignment.

The contract is intentionally smaller than a general modelling language. It does not expose linear
constraints, gradients, or symbolic expressions. That restriction keeps the adapter implementable for
compiled spatial models and public combinatorial libraries, where the objective is often a
domain-specific calculation rather than a closed-form expression. A model that cannot supply exact
evaluation and reversible moves is outside the contract; the harness does not pretend to optimise it.

The assignment representation is a mapping from integer variable positions to integer options. For a
permutation problem, the options are the same integer set as the positions, and the adapter validates
that the mapping is a bijection. For a spatial model, a variable may be a block and an option may be
a treatment or a harvest period; the adapter translates the integer option into the domain value.
The engine never needs to know which interpretation is correct. This is why the same resident-state
and replay code can serve QAP, TSP, and the applied spatial case.

The adapter also supplies \texttt{evaluate\_batch}, which is used by the population layer and by
external baseline adapters. The batch path is not required to be vectorised; it is a contract for
returning exact evaluations, not a performance promise. A vectorised adapter can override it; a
simple adapter can loop over \texttt{evaluate}. The distinction keeps the contract usable for both
small public instances and compiled models.

\subsection{Exact state and replay}

Every reported incumbent is a complete assignment. Evaluation is exact, and the stored assignment must
re-evaluate to the stored objective. The benchmark scripts do not trust the optimizer's internal
counter; they replay the result through the adapter. A record with a mismatched assignment hash or
replayed objective is not evidence.

Replay is more than a checksum. The record names the public instance and its known optimum or
best-known value, the pinned solver version or commit, the seed, the iteration or kick budget, the
stopping reason, the exact-replay result, and the wall and CPU time. A safety-cap hit is recorded as a
cap hit; it is not relabelled as convergence. The replay gate runs the stored assignment through a
new adapter state, so a record cannot pass by reusing the optimizer's cached objective.

The QAP and TSP adapters both validate the assignment before evaluation. QAP checks that the
assignment is a permutation of facilities; TSP checks that it is a permutation of cities. Both return
an objective and a feasibility flag. A malformed assignment raises an error rather than producing a
number. This matters because a permutation repair inside an optimizer can hide an invalid move; the
adapter boundary makes the repair explicit.

\subsection{Framework-managed state}

The engine exposes \texttt{ResidentAssignment}, which holds one complete assignment and routes every
accepted move through the adapter's reversible \texttt{apply}/\texttt{undo} operations, followed by
an exact \texttt{evaluate}. The framework-use experiment repeats the QAP tabu scan and tabu memory
from the reference path, but holds the incumbent in this resident state. The private-array path and
the framework path use the same identity start, seed, delta matrix, tenure rule, patience, and
iteration cap. Execution order is alternated by seed to balance host load; both wall and process CPU
time are recorded.

\subsection{Search layers}

The generic local layer is a simulated annealing search over adapter proposals \citep{kirkpatrick1983}. It evaluates a
proposed move through the resident state, accepts improving moves, and accepts worsening moves with a
temperature-dependent probability. The generic population layer supplies mutation, crossover, and
elitism; the generic layers follow standard metaheuristic designs \citep{talbi2009,pisinger2010}. The
hybrid layer hands a population elite to exact local refinement. These layers are useful
for smoke tests and for new problems that do not yet have a specialised neighbourhood, but they are
not the source of the strongest results in this paper.

The reference implementations are separate adapter-specific modules. The QAP module uses robust tabu
search with an exact delta matrix; the TSP module uses a candidate-list Lin--Kernighan exchange with
double-bridge kicks. The engine still owns the assignment and the replay boundary, but the
neighbourhood and acceptance logic are problem-specific. This is the correct division for a benchmark
harness: a generic engine cannot contain every competitive neighbourhood, and a domain module should
not have to reimplement evidence handling.

\subsection{Evidence protocol}

A reportable result names the pinned solver version or commit, the public instance and known or
best-known value, the seed and budget, the stopping reason, the exact-replay result, and the wall and
CPU time. Publication-facing campaigns use a patience rule; safety-cap hits are recorded as
non-converged.

The evidence format is JSON. Each campaign has per-run records and a summary. The per-run record keeps
the assignment and the search counters; the summary keeps the distribution. The repository also keeps
the runner, the replay script, the instance files or their provenance, and the environment. A reader
can therefore rerun the campaign, replay the stored incumbents, or compare the reported distribution
without reading the paper prose.

Numeric reporting follows one rule. Counts, seeds, budgets, iterations, and runs are exact.
Optimality gaps use three decimal places in percentage points. Campaign objectives use six
significant figures; screen and one-seed diagnostic objectives use three. Times, ratios, and CPU
overheads use three significant figures. Full precision remains in the evidence files.

The target per-run schema contains the instance name, adapter name, seed, budget, stop reason,
objective, feasibility, assignment, exact-replay result, wall time, CPU time, and a metadata block
with the solver commit and hardware. The current files implement this schema unevenly: the
framework-use file carries hardware, worker count, and CPU time; the QAP-expanded, BLS, generic, and
expanded-TSP files carry solver commit, hardware, stop reason, and exact-replay status but not
per-record CPU time; the broad file carries solver commit and a campaign-level replay status but not
per-record hardware or CPU. The campaign-level provenance is in
\url{paper2/evidence/provenance-2026-11-03.json}. Per-record CPU time remains the release blocker. The
validator replays the 40 framework, 20 deep-TSP, 20 large-QAP, 100 BLS, and 40 generic incumbents and
checks the broad/LKH fields; it does not fill missing CPU metadata.

The replay gate is intentionally stricter than the optimizer. It does not call the search code. It
constructs a new adapter, evaluates the stored assignment, and compares the objective and
feasibility. For a permutation problem it also validates the mapping. A record that passes the gate
is reproducible in the narrow sense used here; a record that fails is a defect even if its objective
looks plausible.

\subsection{Implementation and provenance}

The harness is implemented in Python \citep{freshsolutions} and uses NumPy \citep{harris2020} for array operations and SciPy
\citep{virtanen2020} for an independent QAP baseline. The optional accelerated QAP path uses Numba
\citep{lam2015}; the reference path remains available without it. The public instances are QAPLIB
\citep{burkard1997} and TSPLIB \citep{reinelt1991}. The TSA24 applied case is a public UBC-FRESH
artifact \citep{tsa24artifact}. External LKH is a separate research-use binary \citep{lkh3}; it is not
bundled and its results are reported as a capability boundary, not as part of the package.

The campaign-level provenance sidecar records the hardware and software environment, solver commit,
worker counts, and runner commands. The per-run files do not yet uniformly repeat those fields. The
published tables use the pinned public solver version and the public instance files. The evidence release itself is a
candidate until the maintainer approves a tag; the paper does not treat an unapproved candidate as a
published artifact.

\subsection{Artifact structure}

An evidence directory contains the per-run JSON, the summary JSON, the replay script, and the
instance provenance. The QAP framework directory also contains the paired-run runner and the
sensitivity comparison. The TSP directory contains the convergence runner, the 2k and 10k campaigns,
and the replay script. The analysis scripts read the JSON and print the LaTeX table rows, so a
reviewer can regenerate a table without copying numbers from the paper.

The artifact is designed to fail closed. A replay script compares the stored objective with a new
adapter evaluation and raises on a mismatch. A runner records a safety-cap hit rather than silently
dropping the run. A summary records the number of converged runs, not only the mean of the successful
ones. The manifest stores SHA-256 hashes for the evidence files; a tag is not created until the
maintainer approves the candidate.

The public QAPLIB and TSPLIB instances are not redistributed in a modified form. The TSA24 artifact is
published separately with its compiled data and licence statement. External LKH is not bundled; the
provenance sidecar records its version, binary hash, and exact rerun command; the paper treats it as
an external capability reference. This separation matters for licensing: FRESH Solutions code is Apache-2.0, derived artifacts use
CC BY 4.0, and the BC source data carries OGL-BC attribution. The paper-specific evidence repository
is not yet public, so this is an intended release policy, not a claim that the evidence is archived.

\subsection{Failure accounting}

The harness distinguishes four outcomes: plateau stop, safety-cap hit, budget exhaustion, and
invalid replay. A plateau stop is a run that met its declared patience rule. A safety-cap hit is a
run that reached a wall-clock cap before its patience rule; it is not convergence evidence. Budget
exhaustion is a run that reached its iteration or kick cap; it is retained but not called converged.
Invalid replay is a record that cannot reproduce its objective; it is removed from the evidence and
reported as a defect, not averaged into a table.

This taxonomy is simple, but it prevents a common failure mode in heuristic papers: reporting the
converged subset while omitting the runs that hit the cap. The QAP broad panel reports 552 of 670
patience stops and 318 optima; the TSP broad panel reports 236 of 390 patience stops and 195 optima.
The denominator is visible, and the cap hits are not hidden in a footnote.

The QAP convergence panel uses four QAPLIB instances: \texttt{tai12a}, \texttt{tai20a},
\texttt{tai25a}, and \texttt{tai30a}. Each run starts from the adapter's identity assignment, uses a
100,000-iteration patience rule, and has a 1,000,000-iteration cap. A run is reported as
plateau-stopped only when the stop reason is \texttt{patience}; a cap hit is reported as
non-converged. The panel uses ten seeds per instance.

The framework comparison runs two implementations of the same tabu trajectory. The standalone path
keeps the incumbent in a private array and uses Taillard's delta matrix. The framework path keeps the
incumbent in \texttt{ResidentAssignment}, applies every accepted swap through the adapter, and calls
the adapter's exact evaluator. Both use the same start, seed, delta matrix, tenure, patience, and cap.
The order of the two paths is alternated by seed to reduce host-load bias; both wall time and process
CPU time are recorded. The production accelerated path is run as a third check, but its wall time is
not used for the overhead estimate.

The final QAP campaign runs one paired seed per worker process. The pool uses the \texttt{spawn}
start method so that the optional Numba path is not inherited across a fork. Each worker runs the
standalone path, the framework path, and the production path in sequence; the paired ratio is
therefore computed inside one worker on one host, not across different machines. The worker count is
recorded because it affects wall time; the CPU ratio is less sensitive to the number of workers.

The TSP campaign uses the same process-pool design, one seed per worker. The wall time in the TSP
table includes the candidate-list construction and the LK passes; it is not a pure kick count. The
2,000-kick and 10,000-kick-cap campaigns use the same starts and seeds, so the longer campaign is a budget
extension of the same experiment, not a different instance set.

The controlled TSP panel uses \texttt{berlin52}, \texttt{kroA100}, \texttt{kroB100}, and
\texttt{ch150}. Each run starts from the corresponding converged generic local-search tour and uses
10,000 double-bridge kicks with a 1,000-kick patience rule. Five seeds are used. The expanded panel
uses 20 TSPLIB instances and nearest-neighbour starts, with a 3,600-second cap; the 2,000-kick and
20-kick campaigns are retained as shorter-budget checks and are not pooled with the final table.

The broad QAP and TSP panels are separate fixed-budget screens. They use the public QAPLIB and TSPLIB
collections, five seeds, and a 600-second safety cap where stated. A broad-panel run that hits the cap
is reported separately; it is not counted as a converged result. The large-QAP multi-start experiment
uses five random starts per instance. SciPy's quadratic assignment routine
\citep{virtanen2020} is run as a weak, fast external baseline, not as a matched-quality baseline.

\subsection{Instance selection}

The QAP panel spans $n=12$ to $n=30$ and uses instances with published optima or best-known values:
\texttt{tai12a}, \texttt{tai20a}, \texttt{tai25a}, and \texttt{tai30a}. The size range is driven by
the framework comparison: both the private-array and resident-state paths are pure Python at the
trajectory level, and a larger QAP would make the paired comparison a measurement of memory bandwidth
rather than state-management cost. The large-QAP table is run separately with the accelerated path.

The TSP panel spans $n=52$ to $n=150$ and uses four TSPLIB instances with known optima. The starts are
the converged generic local-search tours recorded in the evidence, so the comparison measures the LK and kick layers
from a common starting point. The broad TSP panel covers 78 instances with $n\le1000$; it uses a
different start generator and a fixed budget, so it is reported separately.

The broad QAP panel parses 134 QAPLIB instances; 25 \texttt{esc8} parse-error records (five variants and five seeds) are excluded because the
parser rejects their incomplete format. The parser errors are recorded as parse errors, not as solver
failures. This is a small example of the provenance discipline: a missing benchmark is not a loss.

\subsection{Statistics and provenance}

The summary of each panel reports the mean, median, best, and worst gap, the number of plateau stops
and cap hits, and the wall and CPU times. The framework overhead is the paired ratio of framework to
standalone CPU time; the paper reports the mean and a percentile bootstrap 95\% confidence interval
over seeds. Because the framework and standalone objectives are exactly equal on every seed, a
significance test for a quality difference would be inappropriate.

The paper does not use a performance profile across unlike instance families. A QAP gap normalized by
the known optimum and a TSP gap normalized by the tour length are not on a common scale, and a
profile that mixed them would create a false ranking. The tables therefore keep the families separate
and report the distribution within each family. The same rule applies to time: wall time under a
parallel campaign is descriptive, and the CPU ratio is used only for the paired framework comparison.

Where a table reports a mean, it also reports the worst case or the full denominator. This is a
deliberate response to the heavy tails in the broad panels. A mean without a tail can make a method
look more reliable than it is; a best-of-five without a mean can make a lucky run look like a
portfolio.

The final QAP and TSP campaigns run on a Linux container with 72 logical CPUs and 256 GB of RAM. The
QAP final campaign uses 40 worker processes; the TSP final campaign uses 20. The campaign-level
provenance sidecar records the platform, processor string, Python version, worker counts, solver
commit, and runner commands; the per-run files carry the seed, budget, stop reason, and assignment.
The worker count is part of the provenance because wall times under parallel load are descriptive,
not a single-thread benchmark.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

The first threat is panel size. Four QAP and four TSP instances are enough to test a framework path and
a convergence protocol, but not enough to estimate performance across the full instance
distributions. The broad panels address coverage, but their fixed budgets mean they cannot substitute
for the convergence panels.

The second threat is baseline strength. The QAP reference is robust tabu, a standard but not
state-of-the-art method; SciPy FAQ is weak. The TSP reference is a bounded LK implementation, and
external LKH is stronger on the large tail. The paper therefore reports gaps to known optima and a
capability boundary, not a claim that FRESH Solutions dominates published solvers.

The third threat is timing. The framework comparison is CPU-bound and run in parallel. Alternating
order reduces, but does not eliminate, host-load bias. Process CPU time is the primary overhead
statistic; wall time is reported for operational context.

The fourth threat is stopping. Patience is a plateau rule, not a proof of global optimality. A run can
stop on patience and still be far from the optimum, especially on a deceptive landscape. The paper
uses “plateau-stopped” or “converged for this evidence” rather than “converged” without qualification.

\section{QAP evidence}
\label{sec:qap}

The QAP reference implementation uses robust tabu search over facility swaps. The objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\pi\in\Pi_n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} F_{ij}D_{\pi(i)\pi(j)},
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the flow matrix and $D$ the distance matrix. The adapter uses exact swap deltas; the
accelerated path uses Taillard's delta-matrix update for symmetric zero-diagonal instances and a
Numba general scan for asymmetric instances.

The tabu search scans every facility swap at each iteration and chooses the best non-tabu move. A
move is tabu while its tenure counter is active, unless it would improve the best-known objective;
that aspiration rule prevents the tabu list from blocking a new incumbent. The tenure is sampled
uniformly between $n/2$ and $3n/2$ for a problem of size $n$. The tabu memory is an $n\times n$
matrix, which is small for the panel sizes but becomes a real memory cost for large instances. The
search does not use a long-term frequency memory or a path-relinking layer; it is the robust-tabu
baseline, not a hybrid.

For a candidate swap, the exact objective delta is the sum of the flow--distance contributions of the
two facilities against every other facility, with the diagonal and cross terms handled explicitly.
The adapter computes that delta directly in the reference path. The accelerated path stores the gain
for every pair and updates the matrix after the accepted swap, using Taillard's recurrence for
symmetric zero-diagonal instances. After a swap, the delta matrix is updated in $O(n^2)$ time; the
scan itself is also $O(n^2)$, so one iteration costs $O(n^2)$ for the accelerated symmetric case. The
unaccelerated reference scan computes each swap delta directly and is used as a correctness
fallback.

The tabu search stops on a patience rule or a hard budget. On a plateau stop, the incumbent is the
best assignment seen, not necessarily the current assignment. The best assignment is stored as a
complete mapping, so the replay gate does not need to reconstruct the move history. This is a small
design choice with a large evidence benefit: the record is independent of the tabu memory and the
search counters.

The framework path shares the scan, the tabu memory, and the tenure rule. The difference is the
incumbent representation: the framework path calls \texttt{apply} for the accepted swap and
\texttt{evaluate} for the resulting assignment, while the standalone path mutates a private array
and updates the objective from the delta. The two paths therefore test the cost of routing state
through the adapter, not a different search strategy.

\section{TSP evidence}
\label{sec:tsp}

The TSP reference implementation is a bounded candidate-list Lin--Kernighan-style exchange with
deterministic double-bridge kicks. The starts are converged generic local-search tours; the LK
module does not include a construction layer. For a tour
$\pi$, the objective is
\begin{equation}
L(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{\pi(i),\pi(i+1)},\qquad \pi(n+1)=\pi(1).
\end{equation}

The generic local-search path that produced the starts uses nearest-neighbour starts plus 2-opt and
Or-opt exchanges. The Lin--Kernighan layer keeps a candidate list of the 16 nearest neighbours per city, grows a sequential
exchange to depth four, and accepts a tour only when the complete exchange is improving. The
candidate list bounds the branching factor; the depth bound keeps the bookkeeping finite. A
double-bridge perturbation chooses cuts $a<b<c<d$ and reconnects the segments in the order
$[0,a)\cup[c,d)\cup[b,c)\cup[a,b)\cup[d,n)$, which changes four edges without reversing a long
segment. The perturbed tour is passed back to the LK exchange. The process continues until a kick
budget or a patience rule stops it.

The start tours are deterministic for a fixed seed. They are the converged generic local-search
tours from the earlier campaign, so the TSP table measures the LK and kick layers rather than the
construction path. This is a controlled comparison, not a claim that the
construction is irrelevant. A different start would change the absolute gap and the number of kicks
needed to reach the optimum.

The implementation is a bounded LK-style exchange, not the full LKH method. It does not use
$\alpha$-nearness, fractional cascading, or the full LKH gain criteria. That is why the broad panel
shows a large-instance tail and why external LKH is reported separately. The convergence panel is
chosen to test the evidence protocol and the perturbation schedule, not to claim parity with LKH.

\section{Applied case}
\label{sec:applied}

TSA24/Prince George is a public-data applied case. The frozen build, target, and seed are published as
\texttt{ws4-tsa24-evenflow-v1} in
\url{https://github.com/UBC-FRESH/femic-tsa24-instance}, derived from public BC VRI, FADM, and
SiteProd data. The even-flow target is a WS4 scenario configuration, not an official TSA decision.

The instance combines a compiled spatial model with an operations-research objective. The build
freezes the block geometry, the operable-block set, the yield and treatment assumptions, and the
candidate schedule structure. The target is a maximum-even-flow aspiration with a level term and a
flow term; the objective is lower-is-better. The evidence records the compiled table identity, the
target weights, the seed, and the schedule. A change to any of those invalidates the replay, which is
the point of freezing the artifact rather than regenerating it from a mutable database.

The source data are public BC data with OGL-BC attribution. The compiled artifact is distributed
under the terms recorded in the instance repository. The FRESH Solutions solver is Apache-2.0; the
paper-specific evidence release is pending. This is an intended release policy, not a claim that the
evidence is already archived. The distinction matters because a
public source dataset and a derived compiled model are not the same artifact.

The case has 208,255 operable blocks over a 200-year horizon. The even-flow target is a scenario
configuration, not an official TSA decision. The 100,000-generation GPU-GA campaign spans
512.574--518.621 (mean 515.682) across five seeds. The seed-7 objective decomposes into a level term
of 515.141 and a flow term of 2.721; the realized level is 51.21--52.49M m$^3$ per period against the
70M aspiration. That is a deliberate maximum-even-flow compromise, not a near-zero solution and not a
feasibility failure.

The 1,000-generation record is 8,588,730 and the 10,000-generation same-engine record is
76,540.7; both replay exactly and show that the objective is still moving at short budgets. The
CPU-SA independent-search baseline is $3.35045\times10^8$ after $1.88\times10^3$~s and is time-limited,
not converged. The comparison is therefore a budget illustration, not a matched-performance claim.

The applied case is public-data and reproducible from the artifact, but it is not partner-endorsed
and has no independent model baseline. It is included to show that the adapter and evidence boundary
survive a compiled spatial model, not to claim an operational recommendation.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the harness buys}

The harness makes several engineering properties explicit: the adapter boundary, exact resident state,
replay, convergence records, and benchmark provenance. Those are useful even when the underlying
heuristic is standard. A new problem can reuse the evidence machinery without pretending that a
generic engine contains its best neighbourhood. A new comparison can reuse the replay gate without
trusting a solver's internal objective counter. A reader can inspect a record, rerun a campaign, or
disagree with a stopping rule without reverse-engineering an experimental script.

The strongest QAP and TSP results still come from adapter-specific modules
(\texttt{qap\_tabu.py} and \texttt{tsp\_lk.py}). The harness does not turn those modules into a new
algorithm, and the paper does not present them as state-of-the-art. The contribution is the boundary
that makes their evidence comparable and replayable.

\subsection{Framework use and overhead}

The framework-use result is narrower than a generic search claim: a QAP tabu path held in the engine's
resident state reproduces the private-array trajectory exactly on the tested panel. All 40 runs matched
the standalone and production objectives, stopped on the same patience rule, and replayed exactly.
The measured CPU overhead is 1.24 (95\% bootstrap CI 1.17--1.31); per-instance means range from
1.17 to 1.33. This is a real cost, not a rounding error. It is also small enough to be acceptable
when the alternative is rebuilding replay, provenance, and exact accounting around a new problem.

The overhead has a clear source. The standalone path mutates an array and updates the objective from
the delta; the framework path routes the accepted swap through the adapter and calls the exact
evaluator. The gap is the price of the evidence boundary. A user who needs the last few percent of
single-run speed can keep a private array inside an adapter-specific module. A user who needs
replayable evidence across problems can pay the measured cost and use the resident state.

\subsection{Generic adapter layer: a negative result}

We also built a generic tabu/LNS prototype that uses only adapter proposals, apply/undo, and exact
evaluation. It ran on four QAP and four TSP instances, five seeds, 20,000 iterations, 16 proposals per
iteration, and a 5,000-iteration patience rule. All 40 runs stopped on patience and replayed exactly.
It is not competitive. The QAP mean gap is 3.848\% against 0.097\% for the adapter-specific tabu
reference; the TSP mean gap is 127.978\% against 0.000\% for the adapter-specific LK reference. The
result survives a matched-budget control: tabu at 20,000 iterations averages 0.439\%, and LK at only
2,000 kicks averages 0.004\%.

This is a negative result for the generic search layer, and it is the correct one to report. The
adapter contract is sufficient to run a search; it is not sufficient to replace a problem-specific
neighbourhood. The paper's framework claim is therefore limited to contract portability and
resident-state management, not generic search quality. The generic prototype is retained in the
evidence and is not promoted to a headline result.

\subsection{Stopping rules and benchmark methodology}

The QAP budget comparison is the clearest methodological result. The fixed 10,000-iteration table
reported means of 0.000\%, 0.403\%, 0.786\%, and 0.792\%. The 100,000-patience panel reported 0.000\%,
0.030\%, 0.077\%, and 0.245\%. The difference is not a new algorithm; it is a longer plateau. A paper
that reports only the short budget would understate the method on three instances, while a paper that
reports only the long budget would hide how much work the result required. The harness reports both.

The TSP result makes the same point in a different form. The 20-kick pilot reached the optimum on three of four instances and a
0.383\% mean gap on \texttt{ch150}. The 2,000-kick/200-patience campaign reached 19 of 20 optima. The
10,000-kick-cap/1,000-patience campaign reached all 20. A short-budget table would have been publishable
looking and wrong about the method's ceiling. The convergence table is longer and more expensive, but
it is the honest one.

Patience is still a plateau rule, not global optimality. A deceptive landscape can be flat for a long
time and then improve. The paper therefore says “plateau-stopped” or “converged for this evidence,”
not “proved optimal.” The cap-hit counts are part of the result, not an appendix.

\subsection{Limitations}

The controlled framework panel has four QAP instances. The expanded quality panel has 20, with the
independent BLS baseline; BLS is stronger than robust tabu on that panel, so robust tabu is a
reference implementation, not a state-of-the-art QAP method. The broad panel covers 134 parseable
instances with a fixed budget; it is a screen, not a convergence study. The median gap of 0\% on the
broad panel is dominated by small instances, and the mean is pulled by hard outliers. The
large-instance table is mixed: FRESH tabu wins some instances and loses \texttt{tai150b} to SciPy.

The TSP deep panel has four instances; the expanded panel has 20, with five \texttt{brazil58} cap hits at the
known optimum. The 78-instance broad panel uses nearest-neighbour starts and a different budget; it
is not the same experiment. The internal LK path does not scale to
the largest TSPLIB tail, and the large-tail comparison against LKH is capability-only, not
matched-time. LKH is external research-use software; it is not bundled and is not claimed as FRESH
Solutions' own heuristic.

The applied case is public-data and reproducible from the artifact, but it is not partner-endorsed and
has no independent model baseline. The CPU-SA baseline is time-limited, not converged. The applied
section is an evidence-boundary demonstration, not an operational recommendation.

The generic simulated-annealing and population layers were tested for contract conformance and smoke
behaviour, but they are not competitive with the adapter-specific references in the headline results.
The framework-use experiment covers state management and replay, not a generic search engine. A reader
who wants a general algorithm-selection framework should look elsewhere; a reader who wants a
reproducible adapter and evidence layer is the intended user.

\subsection{Operational implications}

A team adopting the harness should freeze the instance before running the campaign. For a compiled
spatial model, that means recording the table identity, target weights, and candidate set, not just
the database version. For a public library instance, it means recording the file hash and known
optimum. A result produced from a mutable instance is not comparable, even if the seed and solver
version are recorded.

The second operational rule is to separate the search budget from the evidence budget. A short run
can be useful for development, but it should not be promoted to a paper table without the stopping
rule that produced it. The harness records the stop reason for this reason. A reviewer should be able
to see whether a mean came from plateau-stopped runs or from a mixture of plateau stops and cap hits.

The third rule is to replay in continuous integration. A stored objective is not evidence until a
new adapter evaluation reproduces it. The replay gate is cheap relative to the search and catches
serialization, permutation, and objective-accounting defects. It does not catch a bad search design,
but it prevents a bad record from being reported as a good one.

The harness is not the right tool for every project. A team that already has a mature solver and a
mature evidence pipeline should not adopt a new boundary for its own sake. A team that is rebuilding
the same replay and provenance code for every problem is the intended user. The contribution is
useful exactly where the engineering around the heuristic dominates the heuristic itself.

\subsection{What would falsify the claim}

The framework-use claim is narrow enough to falsify. It would fail if any seed produced a different
framework objective from the standalone path, if a run hit the iteration cap, if a stored incumbent
did not replay, or if the paired overhead were so large that the evidence boundary dominated the
search. None of those failures occurred on the 40-run QAP panel: all objectives matched, all runs
stopped on patience, all incumbents replayed, and the mean CPU ratio was 1.24 with an upper
confidence bound of 1.31.

The claim would also fail if a new adapter required changes to the engine's search loop. The adapter
for a new problem would then not be a boundary; it would be a patch. The current QAP and TSP adapters
do not require such changes, but two adapters are not proof of generality. The applied TSA24 case is a separate WS4 codebase, not a FRESH Solutions adapter. It is not part of
the QAP/TSP convergence panel and is not used to claim universal portability.

The TSP convergence claim would fail if the plateau-stopped runs were not reproducible, if the starts
were not the stated local-search tours, or if the 10,000-kick-cap run did not improve on the
2,000-kick run.
The 2k pilot had a 0.077\% worst gap on \texttt{ch150}; the 10k run reached the optimum on all five
seeds. The improvement is evidence that the shorter budget was not converged, and the longer run is
the one reported.

\subsection{Practical guidance and claim boundary}

Use the harness when the expensive part of the project is the model boundary: compiled spatial data,
domain-specific feasibility, exact objective accounting, and evidence that must survive a code review.
Do not use it as an excuse to skip algorithm engineering. The QAP and TSP modules are reference
implementations; a new problem still needs a neighbourhood that fits its structure.

The evidence release and tag remain pending. No state-of-the-art or universal-dominance claim is made.
The public artifact is a candidate until the maintainer approves the release. The paper is written to
be useful before that approval, not to imply that an unreleased artifact is already archived.

\subsection{Future work}

The next evidence step is a wider, family-balanced panel. The QAP side has BLS but still needs more
families and a matched-time comparison; the TSP side has a 20-instance breadth panel but needs a
matched-time comparison against LKH. The applied case needs an independent spatial baseline before
it can support an operational claim.

The next framework step is a portability experiment with a third problem family and an adapter
written by someone other than the reference author. The current QAP and TSP adapters share the contract; the applied TSA24 case is separate. A
third-party adapter is a stronger test of whether the boundary is real. A
containerized artifact and a one-command replay are also needed before the evidence release can be
called archival.

The next algorithmic step is optional. A generic tabu or large-neighbourhood layer could be added to
the engine and tested against the adapter-specific modules, but that is a separate paper. The current
paper deliberately stops at the evidence boundary. Adding a new heuristic to the engine would not
strengthen the framework-use claim unless it were compared fairly and reproduced exactly.

\section{Conclusion}

FRESH Solutions provides a reproducible benchmark harness and adapter boundary, with two reference
metaheuristic implementations, exact replay, and a bounded framework-use result for engine-managed
QAP state. The QAP convergence panel shows that a 100,000-iteration plateau changes the reported gaps
relative to a short fixed budget, while the framework path preserves the search trajectory at a
measured 1.24 CPU ratio. The TSP 10,000-kick-cap campaign closes the gap left by the 20-kick pilot and
reaches all 20 known optima in the convergence panel.

The result is deliberately bounded. The panels are small, the QAP baseline is standard rather than
state-of-the-art, the large TSP tail is weak, and external LKH is a capability reference rather than a
component. The generic simulated-annealing layer is not presented as a competitive search method. The
next step is not a larger claim; it is a wider convergence panel, stronger independent baselines, and
an approved evidence release. The harness is useful now because it makes those next steps replayable.

A reader should be able to reproduce the QAP framework table, the TSP convergence table, and the
replay counts from the committed runners and evidence without reconstructing the experimental
protocol from prose. That is the standard the paper is trying to meet. It is also the standard by
which the next version should be judged.
